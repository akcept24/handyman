What a visit looks like

First I find the dead zones. Then I fix them.

Most home Wi-Fi problems are not the internet plan — they are a router in the wrong corner, factory settings, or one signal trying to cross the whole house. Here is how the visit goes.

What I check and do

  • Walk the home with a signal meter and map where the Wi-Fi actually reaches — and where it dies
  • Check router placement: a router in a corner closet cannot cover a two-story house, and moving it often fixes half the problem
  • Review settings: crowded channels from the neighbors' networks, outdated firmware, band settings that push devices onto the slow band
  • Install and place mesh nodes where they help — not where they look nice — and test again in every room
  • Replace factory passwords, turn off remote admin and other unsafe defaults, and set up a guest network
  • Reconnect the devices that matter: TVs, printer, phones, work computer — and test a video call together before I leave

Honest stop points

  • Running new ethernet cable through walls or the attic: that is cabling work, and I will say so rather than improvise it
  • A dead line or a fault on the provider's side: that is your internet provider's repair, and I will show you the test results to give them
  • If your current router is simply too old for the house, I will tell you plainly — and recommend the exact replacement, not the most expensive one

Not a licensed contractor. Requests are limited to casual, minor work under $1,000 total, including labor and materials, where no permit, employees, or licensed trade is required. Larger projects may not be divided to fit the limit. Other work will be declined or referred.

Before you request

Frequently asked questions

Straight answers about home Wi-Fi work.

Do I need to buy a mesh system before the visit?

No. Tell me the size of your home and where the signal fails, and I will recommend the exact kit to buy — or set up what you already have, if it can do the job. Many homes only need the router moved and set up properly.

My provider gave me a free router. Isn't that enough?

Sometimes — in a small apartment, often yes. In a two-story house, the free router is usually the weak point: it sits where the line enters the home, not where you live. I will test yours honestly and only recommend a replacement if the numbers say so.

Will a mesh system make my internet faster?

Mesh does not change the speed your provider delivers to the house — it delivers that speed to every room. If the couch gets 300 Mbps and the bedroom gets 8, mesh is the fix. If the whole house is slow, that is the plan or the line, and I will show you the difference with tests.

How long does a visit take?

Most Wi-Fi setups and troubleshooting visits take one to two hours, including walking the home and testing together at the end. A mesh installation in a larger home can take a little longer — the price is still the flat price confirmed in writing.

Do you need my Wi-Fi password?

You type passwords yourself — I never ask for, write down, or store them. If we set a new network password together, I leave it with you on a card, and it stays yours.
