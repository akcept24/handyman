Done right, honestly

What a proper setup includes — and where we stop.

A home office is furniture, power, and network in one small space. These rules are how every setup is done.

Always

  • Monitor arms anchored into studs or with proper wall anchors — never just drywall screws
  • Cables in a tray or raceway, one surge-protected strip, nothing pinched or overheating
  • Both monitors matched in height, resolution, and scaling before we call it done
  • Everything tested on a live video call — picture, sound, and screen share — before we leave
  • Anything we supply is at cost, with the receipt handed to you

Honest stop points

  • New outlets or circuit work: that is a licensed electrician’s job — we will say so, not improvise
  • Employer- or school-managed laptops: we build the desk around them; we do not remove their locks or management
  • If your internet plan itself is the bottleneck, we show you the speed test at the router and tell you what to change
  • Glass desks and some wall types limit mounting — we check first and give you the options before drilling anything

Not sure whether your room fits? Send a photo of the corner with the request — we review every request and answer honestly, including when the right answer is “a different specialist.”

Before you request

Frequently asked questions

Straight answers about how a home office setup works.

Do you supply the monitor arms or the desk?

Either way. We install the desk and arms you already bought, or we supply the exact ones at cost and hand you the receipt — no markup. Tell us your monitor sizes in the request and we will recommend the right arms first, so nothing arrives wrong.

Can you assemble a standing desk?

Yes — electric standing desks are included in desk assembly (from $89). We level the frame, route the motor and power cables so nothing snags through the full height range, and test it loaded with your monitors on top.

Can you hide all the cables?

Almost all of them. Monitor, power, and network cables go into a desk tray or raceway with a single surge-protected strip, leaving one tidy run to the wall. Glass desks and a few wall types limit full concealment — we show you the plan before we start, so there are no surprises.

My video calls keep freezing. Can you fix that?

Usually, yes. Most freezes are Wi-Fi, not your computer: we can add a wired drop to the desk or a mesh point in the room (from $119) and prove it on a real call before we leave. The honest limit: if your internet plan itself is too slow or the provider’s line is bad, we will show you the speed test at the router and tell you exactly what to change.

How long does a setup take?

Most home offices take 2–3 hours in one visit. A full bundle with dual monitors and network work can run 3–4 hours. Either way, the scope and the flat price are confirmed in writing before scheduling — the price never grows without your approval.
