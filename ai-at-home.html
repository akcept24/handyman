Useful — and safe

What it can do for you, and what never to share.

Half of this visit is the setup. The other half is judgment: what these tools are genuinely good at, and the lines that keep you safe.

Genuinely useful, every week

  • Letters and emails drafted in your words, then read back to you before you send anything
  • Confusing letters, bills, and documents explained in plain language — paste the text, ask “what does this mean and what do I do?”
  • Trip planning, recipes from what's in the fridge, gift ideas, translation between languages
  • Reminders, lists, and answers to the questions you'd otherwise wait to ask a grandchild
  • You leave with a printed one-page cheat sheet, in large type, with the requests you'll actually use

The safety rules we set together

  • Never type passwords, bank logins, Social Security numbers, or card numbers into any AI chat — it does not need them, ever
  • An AI assistant will never call you, ask for gift cards, or demand urgent payment — anyone who does is a scammer using its name
  • We set the privacy options together: what history is kept, and how to delete a conversation
  • AI can be confidently wrong: for medical, legal, or money decisions, it helps you prepare questions — it does not replace a professional

Before you request

Frequently asked questions

Straight answers about AI setup at home.

Do I have to pay for ChatGPT every month?

No. The free version handles letters, questions, translation, and planning — which is most of what people use it for. A paid plan (about $20 a month) exists for heavier use; if it ever makes sense, it goes on your own account and card, and only if you choose it.

Is it safe? What does the AI see?

It sees only what you type or paste into it — nothing else on your computer. During the visit we set the privacy options together, and I show you how to delete conversations. The hard rule we practice: never type passwords, bank logins, Social Security numbers, or card numbers into any AI chat.

I've never used anything like this. Is it really for me?

Yes — most of my AI setups are for people who describe themselves as “not computer people.” You talk or type the way you'd ask a person. We practice with your real tasks until it feels easy, and the printed cheat sheet stays by your computer for the first weeks.

What computer or phone do I need?

Almost any computer, tablet, or smartphone from the last several years works — it runs in a browser or a free app, nothing to install from a disc. Tell me what you have in the request and I'll confirm before we schedule.

Can it work in Spanish or Russian?

Yes. These assistants understand and answer in Spanish, Russian, and many other languages, and can translate between them — useful for letters to family abroad or documents in two languages. We can set yours up in the language you're most comfortable in.
